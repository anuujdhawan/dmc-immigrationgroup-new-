## SECTION 2 — An Overview of the USA Dependent Visa

> tag: `<h3>`

- `<p>` This program is designed to help families stay together while one member works, studies, or lives in the United States. It allows your loved ones to join you and enjoy a comfortable life. If you are a principal visa holder, your dependents can legally live in the US under your visa category. It gives your family a chance to share the same experience and opportunities.
- `<p>` This visa offers the nonimmigrant status, which means it is connected to the main applicant’s visa type and duration. It ensures that your family can live with you as long as your own visa remains valid. This setup provides emotional stability and peace of mind for those who move to the US for work or education.
- `<p>` Typically, your minor children and spouse are the main applicants under this visa. However, other family members, such as parents or adult dependent children, may also qualify for some specific types of visa.

## SECTION 3 — H-4 Visa

> tag: `<h3>`

- `<p>` It is for dependents of H-1B visa holders. It allows a husband, wife, or child of work visa holders in the country. It may also include limited work authorization options in specific cases.

## SECTION 4 — F-2 Visa

> tag: `<h3>`

- `<p>` You must show evidence of an eligible relationship with the main visa holder. You can submit a marriage or civil partnership certificate. Unmarried partners have to show proof of living together for at least two years. The documents should confirm a genuine and continuing bond.

## SECTION 5 — K-4 Visa

> tag: `<h3>`

- `<p>` It allows children of a spouse visa holder to live in the US while waiting for permanent residence. It is often linked to a US dependent visa for wife or a US dependent visa for husband application.

## SECTION 6 — K-2 Visa

> tag: `<h3>`

- `<p>` It is granted to the child of a K-1 fiancé(e) visa holder. It allows them to travel to the US and later adjust their status after the marriage.

## SECTION 7 — J-2 Visa

> tag: `<h3>`

- `<p>` This visa applies to dependents of J-1 exchange visitors. It gives family members the right to live in the US and even apply for work authorization.

## SECTION 9 — Eligibility Requirements for the USA Dependent Visa

> tag: `<h3>`

`<ul>`
- `<li>` <span>You must have a legitimate relationship with the primary visa holder. If you’re applying as a spouse, you should be legally married or have been living together for a specific period. </span>
- `<li>` <span>If you’re applying as a child, you must be unmarried and under the age of 21. Once you turn 21 or get married, you’re no longer eligible as a dependent.</span>
- `<li>` <span>The principal visa holder must hold a valid visa status, such as an H-1B, F-1, or L-1 visa. They must maintain that status throughout their stay. If the main applicant loses their visa status, your dependent visa will also be affected.</span>
- `<li>` <span>You can either travel with the primary visa holder or join them later. However, you must prove that the principal visa holder is already in the U.S. and maintaining lawful status.</span>
- `<li>` <span>The main visa holder must show sufficient funds to support you during your stay in the United States. Evidence of financial support can include bank statements, salary slips, or sponsorship letters. </span>
- `<li>` <span>You must show that you have no plan to settle permanently in the U.S. unless your visa category allows it. You must demonstrate strong ties to your home country, such as family, property, or employment.</span>
- `<li>` <span>You must have a clean record to be considered eligible. It means no criminal history, previous immigration violations, or fraudulent documentation. The U.S. authorities will assess your background before approving your visa.</span>
- `<li>` <span>If your partner is working in the USA, they must hold a valid Employment Authorization Document (EAD). This card gives them the right to work in the U.S. legally. </span>
`</ul>`
