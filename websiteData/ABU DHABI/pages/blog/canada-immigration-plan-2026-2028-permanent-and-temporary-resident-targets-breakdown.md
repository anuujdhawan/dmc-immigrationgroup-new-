## SECTION 8 — Changes to the distribution of study permits

> tag: `<h2>`

- `<p>` Starting in 2026, the number of international students who are accepted drops by half. Canada wants to have controlled enrollment that better matches the capacity of its institutions.
- `<p>` <strong>What Study Permit Targets Mean:</strong>
- `<p>` <strong>Annual Study Permit Numbers:</strong>
`<ul>`
- `<li>` <strong>Beginning level (2025)</strong>: 305,900 permits given to students from other countries
- `<li>` <strong>First year cut (2026)</strong>: 155,000 permits, which is a 50% cut
- `<li>` <strong>Ongoing levels (2027–2028):</strong> 150,000 permits given out every year
- `<li>` <strong>Main goal: </strong>Ease the strain on housing and campus infrastructure
`</ul>`

## SECTION 9 — Changes to Work Permits and Areas of Focus

> tag: `<h2>`

- `<p>` Overall, the number of work permits issued goes down by about 37% to 40%. Priority shifts toward jobs that require a lot of skill and are hard to fill.
- `<p>` <strong>Annual Allocations for Work Permits</strong>
- `<p>` <strong>Distribution of Work Permits Each Year:</strong>
`<ul>`
- `<li>` <strong>2025 starting point:</strong> 367,750 new work permits approved
- `<li>` <strong>2026 reduced level</strong>: 230,000 work permits available
- `<li>` <strong>2027–2028 ongoing:</strong> 220,000 work permits each year
- `<li>` <strong>Priority sectors:</strong> Healthcare, technology, engineering, and trades that are in high demand
`</ul>`

## SECTION 10 — Help for specific sectors:

> tag: `<h5>`

`<ul>`
- `<li>` <strong>Trade problems:</strong> Industries that are affected by tariffs get targeted help with hiring.
- `<li>` <strong>Focus on geography:</strong> More help with work goes to remote and rural areas.
- `<li>` <strong>Regulation changes:</strong> Temporary jobs with low pay are subject to more rules.
- `<li>` <strong>Skill priorities: </strong>High-skill jobs get better processing treatment.
`</ul>`
